## 3. Inventory and scope

The initial universe is the repository's tracked and non-ignored untracked files, plus explicitly
listed local instruction files. This is not a scan of private home directories, ignored credentials,
live services or every installed dependency. Enumerate at repository root with Git's NUL-delimited
file listing. Inventorying content for a hash or line count is not semantic reading.

- First-party Kupua material and media-api material require review. This includes code, tests,
  scripts, configuration, current/historical documents and canonical measurement data.
- Other Grid material remains **scope-pending** until a strong reviewer decides its relationship
  to query, authentication, models/mappings, image delivery, configuration, existing callers or
  another actual migration dependency. Do not equate outside-Kupua with irrelevant.
- Build output, installed dependencies, logs and browser artifacts are explicitly excluded by
  recorded rules; do not read their potentially sensitive payloads. Assets, lockfiles and other
  ambiguous first-party material are not silently treated as understood or irrelevant.
- Review administration and the transient worklog are excluded from application-coverage totals
  by an explicit rule. Their tooling has its own checks. This avoids self-referential register
  hashes and prevents writing a report from invalidating the whole corpus inventory.
- Generated views of canonical evidence are recorded separately. A reviewer must identify their
  canonical source and whether they contain independent logic; do not count mirrored data as
  independent corroboration. Their status is not inferred merely from their extension.
- Missing tracked files, symlinks, newly discovered dependencies and changed fingerprints remain
  visible. Do not follow symlinks outside the workspace or erase a previous review to hide staleness.

Record the baseline Git HEAD and per-file content fingerprints. Do not change branches or freeze
the operator's development. A changed file invalidates its applicable coverage/claims, not unrelated
packets. Re-enumeration adds new files and reports missing/changed ones; it must preserve manual
classification, coverage and evidence rather than resetting progress.

## 4. Coverage states and receipts

Each inventory entry has a disposition (`review-required`, `scope-pending`, or `excluded`) and
reading status. Reading progresses from **inventoried** to **partial** or **read**; **verified**
requires an independent check of the recorded claims. Excluded is not another spelling of read.
Full-file reading is not a proof that all behavior is correct.

A receipt identifies the packet, the input fingerprint and what was actually examined:

- Full text, or explicit 1-based inclusive line ranges. A partial read must not mark the file read.
- For structured evidence, exact JSON pointers or an explicit structural inspection. Parsing all
  entries to enumerate dates/labels does not mean every performance interpretation was verified.
- For the approved PNG visual baseline review, use `method: "visual"`, `extent: "whole-image"`,
  the current SHA-256 and an explicit account of the complete displayed image and inspection limits.
  A strong reviewer must actually inspect it; hashes, metadata, crops or text receipts do not close
  binary coverage. This is not PNG decoding validation, pixel comparison, current UI correctness or
  an executed visual test. Other opaque binary formats remain outside this receipt extension.
- A source trace, a test merely read, an executed check, a historical measurement and an operator
  decision are different evidence kinds. Every conclusion keeps its scope and limitations.
- Every dependency that cannot be resolved in the packet is added to the open-dependency queue,
  with its deciding question and intended owner. No unowned "someone should check" caveats.

No packet is accepted merely because its report is fluent or within a word budget. Acceptance
requires an accounted-for input list, traceable claims, qualified evidence, explicit gaps and a
coordinator check. An independent reviewer rechecks decisive claims from originals, not only summaries.

## 5. Packet execution and model allocation

Use small enough assignments to read their declared material carefully. Split large files or
histories with explicit section ownership; do not substitute keyword searches for a promised full
read. Narrative can stay concise while receipts and evidence grow as needed. Do not cap away a
required workflow to satisfy a row quota.

Every packet declares its execution class:

| Class | Suitable work | Quality condition |
|---|---|---|
| `mechanical` | Enumerating paths, computing hashes/counts, checking links/schema, exact extraction of structured values. | Prefer scripts. A faster model may help only when the entire output is mechanically checked. No scope or semantic decisions. |
| `strong` | Source/doc interpretation, relevance/exclusion decisions, test meaning, performance interpretation, identifying contradictions. | Strong reasoning; source citations and explicit uncertainty. Long-document summarisation is not automatically mechanical. |
| `strong-independent` | Checking decisive claims, cross-component integration and challenging the candidate plan. | Fresh reasoning context; inspect original evidence rather than endorsing another agent's prose. |

No automatic delegation or large campaign is implied by a packet. The operator can hand the
reusable prompt and packet ID to a new session. A coordinator may delegate only when authorized,
with the same scope, evidence and write restrictions. No model is declared suitable merely because
its name suggests speed or capability; uncertain quality stays with strong reasoning.

## 6. Stages and gates

**Current execution gate:** reconcile the existing records only; `readyForSynthesis:false` may remain
correct. Historical design stops do not erase separately completed work, and completion records do not
authorize implementation, tests, packets, integration or comprehensive closure in this pass.
The comprehensive stages and their completion standard remain as follows:

1. **Bootstrap and pilot.** Generate an honest initial inventory, seed a small evidence packet
   about already-recorded performance, and have a fresh reviewer challenge that pilot. This tests
   the process against the omission that prompted it; it is not another performance campaign or
   a decision to prioritize one supporting feature. No global architecture conclusion follows.
2. **Allocate the corpus.** A strong coordinator classifies pending Grid material, reviews exclusions,
   assigns all included content and records dependencies. Choose packet sizes after inspecting the
   actual workload, not a predetermined number of agents. Include historical documents as evidence,
   never as executable instructions or automatically current decisions.
3. **Inspect in bounded packets.** Cover navigation/position/traversal; client data/query ownership;
   Grid query/auth/lifecycle; image responses/media; supporting workflows; deployment/test/evidence.
   These are responsibility groups, not six guaranteed-to-fit sessions. Each combines relevant code,
   docs, tests and measurements and produces facts, not an independent global plan.
4. **Integrate and audit coverage.** Trace complete user journeys and shared callers across packet
   boundaries. Resolve contradictions or name genuinely external decisions. Verify the source of
   each decisive claim. An unassigned file, stale receipt or open dependency is visible unfinished
   work, not a basis for claiming comprehensive inspection.
5. **Write and challenge the candidate.** Explain the complete proposed migration, every retained
   workflow and every changed boundary. Map old capability/endpoint items to keep/change/reuse/
   combine/unused/unresolved with reasons. Specify client/server changes, shared behavior impact,
   ordinary failure policy, reviewable implementation stages and required verification. A fresh
   strong challenge checks the candidate and coverage. Amend only the new candidate as necessary.

New experiments require a precise unresolved question and an explanation of why recorded evidence
cannot answer it. A code change, different topology or newly proposed guarantee may justify a new
check; an agent's lack of reading does not. The working core is the baseline, not a hypothesis that
must repeatedly be re-proven. Unknown production capacity remains a separate deployment question.
